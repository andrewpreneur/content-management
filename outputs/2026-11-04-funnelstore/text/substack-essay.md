## Sell the Store, Not the Software

Every HighLevel agency hits the same wall.

You've got a platform that can do almost anything. CRM, calendars, automations, AI, reputation, funnels, courses. But when you get on a call with a local business owner and explain all of it, you can watch their eyes glaze over. Therefore you end up discounting, or bundling more, or "following up next week." The deal dies of complexity.

This week HighLevel shipped a small change that's a perfect example of the way out.

### The change

Stores can now be embedded inside an existing funnel or website. You add a single store element in the builder — a cart icon, say — and HighLevel offers to import a theme: which sections of the existing page (hero, gallery, testimonials, FAQ, footer) should carry over onto every store page. Click Import, and the funnel grows the pages a store needs: product list, product details, cart, checkout, thank you.

A one-page funnel becomes a five- or six-page store. Same design, same domain, same CRM.

### Why that matters more than it looks

On its own, this is a convenience. But put it in front of the right buyer and it becomes an offer.

A gym already has a funnel for its free-trial pass. But it sells day passes and shirts at the front desk, in cash, with no follow-up. A coach already has a lead-magnet page. But the workbook she wants to sell lives in a separate tool her CRM can't see. A café already has a website. But it has no way to sell gift cards online in December.

Each of these businesses doesn't want "HighLevel." They want *the thing they sell, sold online, this week.* Therefore the pitch writes itself: "I'll turn your funnel into a store."

### One feature is a business

This is the idea I keep coming back to. You don't need to sell all of HighLevel. You need to sell one feature, to one kind of buyer, with one demo.

- **The feature:** stores inside funnels.
- **Who pays:** local businesses that already have a funnel and something to sell.
- **What to charge:** a setup fee to build it, a monthly fee to run it (new products, promos, abandoned-cart follow-up).
- **How to deliver:** the three-minute setup, then the boring, valuable work of keeping it fresh.

But the hardest part of scaling a one-feature offer isn't the feature. It's onboarding, educating and supporting every client who buys it. That's the problem I built SaaSSuite to solve — one place for your clients, so the tenth store you sell is as easy as the first.

Pick one feature this week. Make it the whole offer. See what happens.

— Andrew
