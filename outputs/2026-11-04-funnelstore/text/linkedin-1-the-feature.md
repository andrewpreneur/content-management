# LinkedIn 1 — the feature (novel-style)
_Note: scenario is illustrative (demo funnel = a gym), not a real client story — swap in a real one if Andrew has it._
_Angle: what changed. Image: carousel slide 1 or the before/after funnel-steps screenshot._

Picture a gym owner who wants an online store.

Not a big one. Day passes, a few shirts, a protein tub.

Until now the answer was usually "Shopify," but that means another login, another bill, and another tool their HighLevel setup can't see.

So open their funnel instead.

One element. A cart icon, dropped into the header. HighLevel asks which sections you want to carry over — hero, gallery, testimonials, FAQ, footer — and you click Import.

Therefore their one-page funnel becomes a store: product list, product details, cart, checkout, thank-you page. Same design. Same domain. Same CRM.

It took less time than this post.

But here's the part most agencies will miss: that's not a feature. That's a product.

"I'll turn your funnel into a store this week" is something a local business will pay for. Setup fee, monthly care plan, done.

You don't need to sell all of HighLevel.

You need to sell one feature.

This is one.

— Andrew George · The One Feature guy · Founder, SaaSSuite
