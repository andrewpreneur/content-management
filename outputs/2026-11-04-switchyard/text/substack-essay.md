# Substack / community essay — draft
## Simplicity Is the Product

There's a moment every HighLevel agency knows. The client logs in for the first time, looks at the screen, and goes quiet.

They see twenty tabs. Pipelines, workflows, reputation, memberships, affiliate manager, AI studio. They came for a calendar and an inbox, but now they're looking at a cockpit. Therefore they close the tab and send you a text instead.

HighLevel's new UI is the first time the platform itself gives you a real fix.

### The change
Inside the new interface, the left-hand navigation has an Edit button. You can reorder tabs, hide whole sections, add quick links to the screens a client actually uses, rename categories and change icons. Then you save that layout and apply it to other sub-accounts.

You decide what your client sees.

### Why it matters
"Too complicated" has always been HighLevel's biggest objection. You couldn't change the software, so you added training: Looms, help docs, onboarding calls. All useful. But the screen stayed the same.

Now the screen can change. A med spa gets Conversations, Calendar, Contacts and Reviews. A roofer gets Opportunities and the inbox. Nobody gets Affiliate Manager unless they asked for it.

### One feature is a business
- **The feature:** custom navigation layouts.
- **Who pays:** agencies with many clients in one niche, white-label SaaS builders, consultants with client portals.
- **What it sells:** "HighLevel, built for your industry." Fewer support tickets. Clients who log in.
- **How to deliver:** design one layout per niche, save it, apply it everywhere.

But a clean menu is only the first step. Clients still need to be onboarded, taught and supported. That's what I built SaaSSuite for — one place for your clients.

Pick one niche. Design one layout. Hide everything else.

— Andrew
