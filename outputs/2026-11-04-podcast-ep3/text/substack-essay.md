## Positive Is Not Payment

Every salesperson has a graveyard of great calls.

The prospect laughed at your jokes. They agreed with everything. They said "we're ready." But then they needed time to think, and time turned into silence.

I spent my first years of sales in that graveyard, so I wrote myself a rule book. I called it Positive to Payment, and I recently walked through it on my podcast.

### Deposits before withdrawals
Every relationship has a give-to-ask ratio. Most people ask too soon, therefore the ask feels like a withdrawal from an account that was never funded. I call the balance perceived relationship equity. Your job on a first call isn't to pitch. It's to make deposits: be useful, listen, make them feel understood.

### Clarity: three conditions
People pay when they (1) understand they have a problem, (2) trust you to fix it, and (3) have a way to pay you. The second one is where transparency wins. "We can fix your website" means nothing. "Here's how we'll fix it, and who does what" means everything. The third one sounds obvious, but it's where most "yes" calls die: the payment link isn't ready.

### Commitment
The more someone invests in the relationship — signing up for your lead magnet, watching your video, replying to your emails — the more likely they are to pay. That's why I like engagement scores: they turn "they seemed interested" into a number.

### Pay live, then reinforce
If they're in, send the link and stay with them while they pay. If that feels high pressure, you skipped a step. And after they pay, reinforce the decision. What you do in the hour after the sale protects the sale.

A positive reaction is a feeling. A payment is a decision. Build the bridge between them on purpose.

— Andrew
